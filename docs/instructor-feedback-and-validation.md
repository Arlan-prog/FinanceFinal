# Instructor feedback and next validation steps

Added on 15 November 2026 after submission. This note supplements the repository; it does not replace the submitted report, presentation or financial workbook. Those files retain their submitted assumptions and results. The limitations below remain unresolved by empirical evidence.

## Limitations of the submitted forecast

- **Customer acquisition:** the revenue forecast does not establish a measured path from marketing spending to qualified leads, conversion and paying customers. Projected volumes are planning assumptions, not validated demand.
- **Market size:** the cited figures do not adequately establish the reachable rental-deposit market. Housing stock, rental contracts, landlords and paying agencies are different quantities and cannot be substituted for one another.
- **B2B subscriptions:** willingness to pay, customer acquisition cost, churn and subscription growth have not been validated. Treating subscribers as paying for all twelve months can overstate first-year revenue when customers join during the year.
- **Sustainability:** the submitted financial model shows material funding and viability weaknesses. A hypothetical investment request or equity allocation does not demonstrate that the business can become sustainable.

No actual customer survey responses, paid-pilot results or advertising conversion data have been supplied for this project. No invented survey responses are presented as evidence. The working Solidity prototype demonstrates technical behaviour only; it does not validate commercial demand or bank integration.

## Methodology for a future revision

1. Define a narrow launch segment and document dated primary sources, geographic scope, units and exclusions. Use housing statistics only as context unless rental frequency and eligibility are independently supported. Do not infer paying B2B organisations from the number of dwellings.
2. Conduct consent-based exploratory interviews with landlords and agencies. Record anonymised sample selection, responses, objections and price reactions. Report the sample size and selection bias; do not call a convenience sample representative.
3. Test acquisition channels with a capped budget. Measure cost per qualified lead, conversion to a funded contract, and distinct paying customers. Separate direct customers from agency-generated contracts to prevent double counting.
4. Build monthly B2B cohorts: qualified organisations → trials → paid accounts, with sales-cycle delays, churn and billing start dates. Calculate revenue from paid account-months, rather than year-end subscribers multiplied by twelve. Test willingness to pay before extrapolating growth.
5. Reconcile marketing and sales budgets with lead volumes and staff capacity. Calculate acquisition cost including attributable sales labour, contribution after variable costs, retention-adjusted customer value and payback. Distinguish embedded staff allocations from additional costs to avoid double counting expenses.
6. Recalculate cash flow, funding gap and sensitivity, including lower conversion, zero B2B revenue and combined cost/demand stress. Show a no-launch outcome when unit economics or financing remain inadequate; do not change assumptions merely to produce profitability.
7. Consider a small, separately budgeted demand-validation stage before a full launch. Define stop/go criteria in advance. Such a study is not a funded operating business or evidence of future profitability.

A future model should label each input as observed evidence, an external benchmark or an unvalidated hypothesis, with its source and date. All linked reports, slides and defense materials would then need consistent revision. No replacement forecast is adopted by this note.
